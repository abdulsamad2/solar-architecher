Scope note: these notes were written on 2026-11-12. They start from the manuscript's current state: `paper/qc/v5_submission_readiness.md` §C (items C1, C2 and C7) and `reports/phaseA_report.md` (A1/A2). Local facts are labelled "(local report)". ## Q1. Which published probabilistic methods exist, and what were their protocols (reference, horizon, test set, m, CRPS estimator, Winkler definition, calibration, values)?

### Takeaway
None of the probabilistic comparators is scored on the official SKIPP'D 20-day test set.
- SkyGPT and PV-MM-Diffusion use SkyGPT's own split, built from a pre-release in-house version of the Stanford data: 2,582 test samples from 5 cloudy days in Nov–Dec 2019.
- They score different numbers of samples: SkyGPT uses m = 100 (10 video futures × 10 U-Net folds), PV-MM-Diffusion uses m = 30, and SUNSET and the "real future image" oracle use m = 10 (10 CV folds).
- CRPS is the empirical sample CRPS, without the "fair" correction. Winkler is computed at 90% nominal from raw 5th/95th sample percentiles, with no calibration step.

These numbers can therefore only be compared contextually and without pairing. Xiong et al. 2025 could not be verified at the full-text level.

### Cited Findings
**SkyGPT (Nie et al.)**
- **Reference.** Y. Nie, E. Zelikman, A. Scott, Q. Paletta, A. Brandt, "SkyGPT: Probabilistic ultra-short-term solar forecasting using synthetic sky images from physics-constrained VideoGPT", *Advances in Applied Energy* (2024), doi:10.1016/j.adapen.2024.100172.
  - Preprint: arXiv:2306.11682 (doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11682), whose title says "Synthetic Sky Videos".
  - Sources: [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=SkyGPT); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11682).
  - The journal page numbers or article number were not displayed in the Crossref query output. Check against the publisher page.
- **Horizon.** 15-min-ahead PV output for a 30-kW rooftop PV system. — [arXiv:2306.11682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11682)
- **Data and test set.** The data are not the official SKIPP'D release. The paper says: "This study was conducted before the official release of our curated dataset SKIPP'D … which is more organized and has a number of updates from the dataset we used". — [arXiv HTML v1, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
  - 10 cloudy validation days were hand-picked from Mar 2017 to Oct 2019; the rest of that period is training.
  - The split gives 53,336 / 4,467 / 2,582 samples for training / validation / test. — [arXiv HTML v1, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
  - The test set is 5 cloudy days: 2019-11-12, 11-13, 11-29, 12-08 and 12-23 (local report, from SkyGPT's public `test_set_2019nov_dec.hdf5`).
- **Number of samples m.** "For the stochastic video prediction models, i.e., VideoGPT and SkyGPT, 10 possible futures are generated for each historical sky image sequence and fed to 10 U-Net sub-models, thus, 100 predictions in total are generated." — [arXiv HTML v1, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
  - SUNSET and "Real future SI → U-Net" "can generate 10 predictions due to 10-fold cross-validation". Smart persistence has 1 prediction, so its CRPS reduces to MAE. — [same](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
- **CRPS estimator.** CRPS is defined as the integral of [F_target(x) − F_model(x)]², where F_model is "the cumulative distribution of the PV output predictions generated by the model" and F_target is a Heaviside step at the observation. It is averaged over N samples. Gneiting & Katzfuss 2014 is cited. — [arXiv HTML v1, Eq. 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
  - The empirical CDF of the m predictions is used, so this equals the standard ("unfair") energy-form estimator.
  - No fair/ensemble-size correction is mentioned.
- **Winkler score.** It follows Winkler 1972: WS = δ + 2(L−x)/α if x < L, δ + 2(x−U)/α if x > U, and δ otherwise, where δ = U − L. — [arXiv HTML v1, Eq. 14](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
  - "we use 95th percentile and 5th percentile of the predictions for U_k and L_k … nominal confidence level of 90% (i.e., α = 0.1)". These are raw sample percentiles; no calibration step is described. — [same](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
- **Reported values.** Table 1 gives validation CRPS (FS), WS and then test CRPS (FS), WS. — [arXiv HTML v1, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)
  - SkyGPT→U-Net: val 2.49 (14%), 26.72; test 2.81 (23%), 26.70.
  - VideoGPT→U-Net: test 3.34, 36.22.
  - PhyDNet+GAN→U-Net: test 3.02, 39.54.
  - Test values for SUNSET (3.31, 56.95) and smart persistence (3.67) appear in the same table (local report cross-check). The abstract says 2.81 is "13% better than SUNSET and 23% better than smart persistence". — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11682)
- **Choice of m.** The paper argues "10 future scenarios is a good choice that balances probabilistic solar forecasting performance and computational cost". — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11682)

**PV-MM-Diffusion (Huang et al.)**

- **Protocol (local report, from the full text the authors supplied).** All methods are evaluated "on the official validation set (4,467 samples from 10 cloudy days) and test set (2,582 samples from 5 cloudy days) following the SkyGPT split" (§3.3, §4.2).
  - It uses 30 diffusion samples per input.
  - Table 3, test set (CRPS / WS90):
    - PV-MM-Diffusion: 2.63 / 21.46
    - Real-future-image → U-Net oracle: 2.52 / 30.72
    - QR-GRU: 2.95 / 33.38
    - MC-Dropout SUNSET: 3.10 / 43.68
    - SkyGPT → U-Net re-run: 3.21 / 35.62
    - SUNSET re-run: 3.28 / 56.29
    - Persistence: 3.67
  - Cost: 45.4 s per forecast for 10 samples on an RTX 3090.
  - These facts are not independently re-verified here; the publisher full text is paywalled.

### Inferences
- **Different m inflates CRPS by different amounts.** The empirical sample CRPS is (1/m)Σ|x_i − y| − (1/(2m²))Σ_iΣ_j|x_i − x_j|. In expectation it exceeds the CRPS of the generating distribution by E|X − X′|/(2m) (Ferro 2014; Zamo & Naveau 2018; see Q3).
  - At equal ensemble spread, the inflation at m = 10 (SUNSET, oracle) is 10× that at m = 100 (SkyGPT) and 3× that at m = 30 (PV-MM-Diffusion).
  - The oracle's 2.52 at m = 10 is therefore not like-for-like with m = 30 or m = 100 rows.
  - The manuscript's own check shows this effect: 2.49 at m = 30 vs 2.59 at m = 10 (local report).
- **Defensible framing for Appendix C.**
  - Population: "same 2,582-sample SkyGPT test population (5 cloudy days, Nov–Dec 2019), not the SKIPP'D 20-day test".
  - Pairing: unpaired, because per-sample competitor predictions are not public.
  - Estimators: report each competitor's m and estimator. Report the manuscript's CRPS both at matched m (10/30/100 samples drawn from its predictive quantiles) and with the fair estimator.
- **Raw intervals.** All published WS90 values are raw 5–95% sample-percentile intervals, with no conformal or other recalibration described in SkyGPT. The manuscript's calibrated (conformal) intervals should be reported in a separate row.
- **Choosing m = 30 (C7).** m = 30 matches PV-MM-Diffusion, the strongest published comparator, and lies between SUNSET's m = 10 and SkyGPT's m = 100. Reporting at m ∈ {10, 30, 100} plus the fair estimator answers the reviewer more fully than a single m.

## Q2. What are the key conformal / adaptive conformal references for time series, and solar applications 2022–2026?

### Takeaway
The core references are:
- **Gibbs & Candès 2021 (ACI):** an online α_t update.
- **Zaffran et al. 2022 (AgACI):** online aggregation of ACI experts with different step sizes; its application was electricity price forecasting.
- **Barber et al. 2023 (nonexchangeable conformal, weighted with a coverage-gap bound):** Ann. Statist.
- **Angelopoulos, Candès & Tibshirani 2023 (conformal PID):** NeurIPS.

Solar applications are mostly split / ensemble conformal on tabular PV data. No adaptive-conformal application to sky-image nowcasting on SKIPP'D was found.

### Cited Findings

  - Crossref did not return a DOI. NeurIPS 2021 proceedings usually lack DOIs.
  - Volume and pages (NeurIPS 34) were not verified here.
- **Zaffran, M., Dieuleveut, A., Féron, O., Goude, Y., Josse, J.**, "Adaptive Conformal Predictions for Time Series", ICML 2022, arXiv:2202.07282. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07282)
  - The PMLR volume 162 page range was not verified in this session.
- **Barber, R.F., Candès, E.J., Ramdas, A., Tibshirani, R.J.**, "Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability", *Ann. Statist.* 51(2):816–845 (2023), doi:10.1214/23-AOS2276; arXiv:2202.13415. — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-aos2276); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415)
- **Angelopoulos, A.N., Candès, E.J., Tibshirani, R.J.**, "Conformal PID Control for Time Series Prediction", NeurIPS 36 (2023) pp. 23047–23074, doi:10.52202/075280-1000; arXiv:2307.16895. — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-1000); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16895)
- **Solar application, day-ahead PV:** "Enhancing the reliability of probabilistic PV power forecasts using conformal prediction", *Solar Energy Advances* 4 (2024) 100059, doi:10.1016/j.seja.2024.100059 (open access). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667113124000093)
  - Uses day-ahead PV from NWP inputs, in the Netherlands.
  - Methods: weighted CP, CP with KNN, Mondrian binning, and conformal predictive systems.
  - CP + KNN + Mondrian after random forest improves the weighted interval score by 14% vs multiple linear quantile regression.
- **Solar application, very short term:** "Comparative analysis of conformal prediction techniques and machine learning models for very short-term solar power forecasting", *Energy and AI* 21 (2025) 100573, doi:10.1016/j.egyai.2025.100573 (open access). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666546825001053)
  - Methods: ICP, J+aB and EnbPI with RF, XGBoost, LSTM and Transformer.
  - Horizons: 5, 10 and 15 min.
  - Sites: Ninh Thuan (Vietnam) and SolarTechLAB (PoliMi, Italy).
  - LSTM gives "overly narrow intervals with significant undercoverage", and the paper highlights "interval calibration" and "temporal segmentation".
- A 2026 arXiv preprint, "Spectral Similarity Weights for Adaptive Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2606.15950v3), sums up the literature this way: ACI, AgACI and conformal PID "repair the long-run miss rate, but they do not say which past residuals are relevant". It contrasts these with recency/similarity weighting (Tibshirani 2019; Barber 2023). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15950v3)

### Inferences
- **Explaining the manuscript's Phase-A failure.** The frozen causal rolling conformal method reached 77% coverage at 90% nominal on the SkyGPT winter days (local report). This is the failure mode ACI-type methods target: residuals from late-October development data under-represent winter cloudy volatility.
  - ACI/AgACI only guarantees long-run average coverage. On 5 days with a daily reset it may still under-cover within a day.
  - Citing Gibbs & Candès plus Zaffran (and Barber 2023 for weighting) makes this limitation explicit, rather than presenting calibrated intervals as guaranteed.
- The solar CP papers found use tabular inputs and split/EnbPI-style CP. They make suitable "CP has been applied to PV" citations, but they are not sky-image comparators.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed application of ACI, AgACI or conformal PID specifically to sky-image PV nowcasting was found within the search budget. This absence is not exhaustive.
- The authors of the two solar CP papers were not extracted, because ScienceDirect did not render author names. Fill these in from the DOIs.
- Gibbs & Candès (NeurIPS 34 pages) and Zaffran (PMLR 162 pages) proceedings details need a check before citation.

## Q3. What are the references for the fair CRPS estimator?

### Takeaway
Ferro (2014, QJRMS) defines "fair" ensemble scores, including the fair CRPS with 1/(2m(m−1)) normalisation of the spread term, which removes the dependence on ensemble size. Zamo & Naveau (2018, Math. Geosci.) compare CRPS estimators when only an ensemble or quantiles are available. Together they justify reporting a fair, m-matched CRPS.

### Cited Findings
- **Ferro, C.A.T.**, "Fair scores for ensemble forecasts", *Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc.* 140 (2014) 1917–1923, doi:10.1002/qj.2270. Crossref's issued date is 2013-12-03 (online first); the volume is the 2014 issue. — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2270)
- **Zamo, M., Naveau, P.**, "Estimation of the Continuous Ranked Probability Score with Limited Information and Applications to Ensemble Weather Forecasts", *Math. Geosci.* 50 (2018) 209–234, doi:10.1007/s11004-017-9709-7 (online 2017-11-21). — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-017-9709-7)
- **CRPS definition and the property that it reduces to MAE for point forecasts:** Gneiting & Katzfuss 2014, as cited by SkyGPT. — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11682v1)

### Inferences
- **The two estimators.**
  - Standard (empirical/energy, "PWM-like") estimator: CRPS_m = (1/m)Σ|x_i − y| − (1/(2m²))ΣΣ|x_i − x_j|.
  - Fair estimator: replaces 1/(2m²) with 1/(2m(m−1)).
  - The gap between them is (1/(2m²(m−1)))ΣΣ|x_i − x_j|. This is about mean-pairwise-spread/(2m), so about 5% of the spread at m = 10 and about 0.5% at m = 100.
  - The manuscript can compute this exactly for its own samples. It cannot compute it for competitors, because their per-sample spreads are unpublished.
- **The manuscript's 9-level quantile-interpolation CRPS** is a third estimator type, of the quantile-based kind that Zamo & Naveau discuss. Appendix C should name it explicitly. The local check found it 1.2% higher than the empirical estimator on SUNSET's 10 members.

### Gaps
- The exact equation numbers in Ferro 2014 and Zamo & Naveau 2018 were not retrieved; both are paywalled. The formula above is the standard form, and the equations should be cited from the papers after checking.
